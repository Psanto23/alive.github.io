The Castle

Unbeknownst to the white picket fences, schoolchildren and their buttoned up parents, 
stands a dark shadow of solitude on the corner of Lasalle. Tread lightly, for the ground you walk upon is haunted by ghosts who practice the ritual of a paranoid return, who still lock the paint chipped doors, set the alarm system for a house that no one, let alone an intruder, visits.

What’s your interest? Are you one of the scavengers, scouring for profits among decade old newspapers, discarded receipts from 1993, and a perfectly preserved tv dinner. Or perhaps a member of the unfortunate few tasked with extracting each crate of collected memories for disposal? Regardless

Enter at your own risk

These halls rot a soured stench of spoiled desires that seep into your skull, stretching the skin of your scalp against the membrane of soft white matter underneath. Every particle of air seems to stick, holding onto you, dearly, sweetly, caressing you into a comatose. Shaded lamps whimper and wane in the face of the dark damp caverns of the Castle. 

Under layers of snow-like dust are the trinkets and tokens that encompass a man that could have and should have but was swallowed by his own stubborn demeanor. Inked away in the cupboards of two ton file cabinets lay a library of personal literature, so finely detailed, that you could nearly blotch a sketch of his daily routine. 

If you choose to go farther and venture into the depth of the catacombs below, the soldiers of shame and worry will begin to engulf you. Crawling out of the crevices of cement and cinderblock, like leeches, they latch on to you, exercising their will. Softly whispering in the darkness:

You too. You too, will hold on forever, thinking it can save you, thinking it can shield you from your pain.
